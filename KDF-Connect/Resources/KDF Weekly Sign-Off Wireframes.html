Case Study · KDF Connect · Weekly Sign-Off · 2021

Forty-four names, one week, one signature — and no quiet edits after it's drawn.

Wireframing the Weekly Sign-Off flow — the screen that turns a week of hours into a record nobody can walk back.

At KDF, a week's timesheet became "official" when a supervisor said so — verbally, or with a signature on a paper stack that reached the office days later. There was no single moment where a week was actually done.

The wireframe had to design that moment twice: a screen where a supervisor reviews every employee's hours, and a harder screen — the one that asks for a signature and locks the record the instant it's given.

01

Zoning two screens, not one

Reviewing a week and committing to it are different mental modes. They needed different chrome.

Information architecture

What this pass settled

  • List screen and confirmation modal are two distinct surfaces — browsing and committing don't share one long form.
  • Submit is a single, persistent action on the list — the week gets signed off once, even though each row can be reviewed individually first.
  • The modal's three summary numbers sit above the fold — nobody scrolls to find what they're approving.
  • Signature sits last, structurally below the numbers it depends on.
  • Cancel and Submit get equal size — backing out should never feel harder than committing.

02

What happens if someone's unsigned?

A real week always has a holdout. Three ways to handle Submit when not everyone's approved.

Edge-case handling
A
Hard block. Submit stays disabled until 100% are signed.
+ Guarantees a complete record. − One late timesheet holds the whole crew's pay hostage.
B
Soft warning. "Continue with unsigned employees?"
+ Keeps the supervisor deciding, every time. Chosen →
C
Silent submit. Send it, flag gaps later in the admin dashboard.
+ Never blocks the supervisor. − Moves the "did everyone get reviewed" call to someone who wasn't there.

Why B won

A hard block (A) treats "100% signed" as a precondition the app enforces — but paper never enforced that, and demanding perfection from day one would have taught supervisors to fight the tool instead of trust it. Silent submit (C) is worse: it removes the one moment a human actually decides whether an unsigned row is fine to let through, and hands that judgment to someone in the office who wasn't on-site that week. B keeps the decision where it belongs — with the supervisor, at the exact moment they'd notice it anyway — and makes it a deliberate tap, not a default.

From wireframe to React Native.

From this wireframe, the Weekly Sign-Off flow shipped as EmployeeSignOffList for the review and SubmitTimesheetModal for the commit, with react-native-signature-canvas capturing the actual signature. The "continue with unsigned employees?" warning shipped exactly where the wireframe put it — one screen up from the modal, in the supervisor's hands, not the app's.

Once submitted, the mutation stamps an approval timestamp and locks the record server-side — the same signature that replaced a paper form also replaced the ambiguity about when a week was actually done.
